How to use it
Use the copy verbatim. Do not paraphrase, and do not write your own headings.
Square brackets [like this] are variables to interpolate.
Where a screen needs a string that is not here, ask rather than inventing one — particularly for anything making a claim about content, accuracy or clinical outcomes.

1.2 Voice
Intelligent, calm, precise, supportive. Encouraging but never patronising. Plain British English.
Spelling and conventions: British English throughout — organise, personalised, analyse, programme. Use “medicines” not “meds”. Write micrograms in full, never “mcg” or “µg”. Use the 24-hour clock nowhere; use “3:00 pm” style. Dates as “18 September 2027”.
1.3 Banned phrases
Never write, anywhere in the product:
“You failed”
“You are falling behind”
“Only [X] days left”
“Don’t lose your streak”
“We miss you!”
“Wrong!”
“Mastered” (as a progress status)
“Guaranteed”
“Everything you need to pass”
“Complete curriculum coverage”
Any claim of endorsement by the GPhC, NHS, NICE, a university or an examining body
“AI-powered” as a standalone selling point — say what it does, not what it runs on
Any wording implying Ace is a clinician, has clinical authority, or has been individually checked by a named pharmacist

24. Weekly insight
Heading: This week Supporting line: A written read on your week, not another chart. Panel subtitle: Written from your first-attempt data
Structure of the paragraph — assemble in this order, omitting anything the data does not support:
You answered [N] questions this week at [X]% first-attempt accuracy.
[Topic] is your weakest area at [X]%. [Topic] is holding up well at [Y]%.
Confidence — lead with this whenever it exists: > The thing worth your attention: [N] questions you felt confident about were wrong. That gap between what you think you know and what you do know is the most useful signal in your data — and it is the one you cannot see without being asked for your confidence.
Or, where confidence tracked performance: > Your confidence ratings tracked your actual performance closely this week, which suggests you have a realistic picture of what you know.
One thing for next week: a 15-question [Topic] session early in the week, while you have the attention for it.
Quick actions: Build me a plan around this · Start that session now · Compare with last month
Empty state > Nothing to report yet > Complete a session and Ace will write you a summary.
Explainer > Why a paragraph, not a dashboard. Students already have access to their numbers. What they usually cannot do is interpret them — decide which of six percentages actually deserves this week’s attention. That interpretation is the thing worth automating.
